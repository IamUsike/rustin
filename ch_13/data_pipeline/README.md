_No Longer Human_ is heavily inspired by Osamu Dazai's own life. The story follows Yozo Oba, a man who feels profoundly disconnected from society and incapable of understanding other people. Though he often appears cheerful, humorous, and popular, this is merely a mask concealing the emptiness and fear within him.

Yozo constantly tries to please those around him, not out of kindness, but out of terror. He is afraid of being exposed, afraid of being misunderstood, and afraid of rejection. This pathological people-pleasing gradually sends him into a downward spiral from which he seems unable to recover.

The novel takes us through the highs and lows of a deeply troubled individual. Knowing that much of it draws from Dazai's own experiences makes many of the anecdotes feel even more impactful. At times I felt compassion for Yozo; at others, I pitied him and found myself thinking, "It's not that complicated!" as he continued to make destructive choices. Yet the story repeatedly reminds us that things are rarely that simple.

As the novel progresses, Yozo descends into addiction, deception, and self-destruction, forcing readers to question what it truly means to be human. _No Longer Human_ offers a nuanced perspective on alienation, identity, and suffering, while presenting a deeply intimate portrait of a very human person.
